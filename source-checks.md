This record describes passages actually inspected, not full-book verification.

## Whitehead (1967 [1933]), Adventures of Ideas

Read the title/copyright pages and Chapter XI, sections 1–4, printed pp. 175–177
in the [Free Press scan](https://s3.amazonaws.com/arena-attachments/270366/f3f6d5cfe69f655b1a7020aba868ef24.pdf).
The short quotation is on p. 176. The text makes affect central; it does not
equate concern with signal coupling. The paper now distinguishes its engineering
definition from Whitehead's account. No claim to reading the whole book.

## Noddings (2002), Starting at Home

Checked [publisher metadata](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/starting-at-home/paper)
and read the opening discussion and reception/response passages of
[Chapter 1](https://content.ucpress.edu/title/9780520230262/9780520230262_noddings_chapter_one.pdf),

The rest of the book was not inspected.

## Ay, Jost, Lê and Schwachhöfer (2015)

Read the abstract, introduction, and finite-model characterization with its
preceding discussion (Proposition 3.19) in
[arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1207.6736v4).
Checked the [journal record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00440-014-0574-8):
online 2014, volume 162 in 2015, pp. 327–364.
The relevant uniqueness claim is conditional on invariant metric families across
models. It does not compel a welfare representation or moral metric. We do not
claim to have audited the full infinite-dimensional proof.

## Canguilhem (1991), The Normal and the Pathological

Checked the [Zone edition](https://www.zonebooks.org/books/81-the-normal-and-the-pathological).
Read the disease/normative-capacity discussion around pp. 181–187 and the
conclusion around pp. 226–228 in the
[primary-text scan](https://monoskop.org/images/b/b6/Canguilhem_Georges_The_Normal_and_the_Pathologic_1991.pdf).
The former “single norm” wording is replaced by a bounded paraphrase.
Equating normative capacity with entropy over fixed outcome labels is our
rejected modeling step, not Canguilhem's proposition. The MIT mirror returned
404; the accessible scan was read through the document browser. No full-book read.

## Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky and Armstrong (2015), Corrigibility

Read the abstract, initial motivation, section 2 desiderata and sections 4–5
in the [authors' PDF](https://intelligence.org/files/Corrigibility.pdf).
The current comparison is limited to correction, incentives, propagation of
constraints and safe shutdown. Our gate solves none of the incentive problems.
Author order follows the PDF's grouped byline and the
[institute's publication list](https://intelligence.org/all-publications/);
some indexes flatten the affiliations differently.
